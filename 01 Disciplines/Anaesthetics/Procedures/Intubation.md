# Indications
- A – protection and patency (e.g. against aspiration or relief of upper airway obstruction as in anaphylaxis or head and neck trauma)
- B – respiratory failure (hypercapnic or hypoxic), increase FRC, decrease WOB, secretion management/ pulmonary toilet, to facilitate bronchoscopy  
- C – minimise oxygen consumption and optimize oxygen delivery (e.g. sepsis)  
- D – unresponsive to pain, terminate seizure, prevent secondary brain injury  
- E — temperature control (e.g. serotonin syndrome)
# Complications
- A – CICV, failed intubation, damage at intubation, mouth ulceration and bleeding  
- B – VAP, VILI, barotraumas, oxygen toxicity, patient ventilator asynchrony  
- C – decreased RV preload and increased RV afterload, decreased splanchnic blood flow, increased ICP, fluid retention due to decreased Q, decreased renal blood flow  
- D – neuropsychiatric complications, insomnia, pain, weakness, PTSD
# Preoxygenation
- The main goal is to extend the safe apnoea time primarily by denitrogenation of the lungs and is maximally achieved when alveolar, arterial, tissue and venous compartments are all filled with oxygen
- End tidal $\ce{O2}$ (ETO2) monitoring is the gold standard test for assessing denitrogentation 
	- Optimal pre-oxygenation is achieved when ETO2 = 90%, but not that this may not be achievable in some critically ill patients
	- Note that a $S_\text{p}\ce{O2}$ of 100% does not indicate optimal pre-oxygenation (because haemoglobin becomes 100% saturated at a $P_\text{a}\ce{O2}$ only slightly above that provided by room air)
		- In critically ill patients, the lag between $S_\text{p}\ce{O2}$ and adequate pre-oxygenation may be >90 seconds, but if no improvement after 3-4 minutes, proceed with intubation
- Safe apnoea time refers to the duration of time until critical desaturation occurs following cessation of breathing/ventilation which is at about $S_\text{a}\ce{O2}$ 88-90%
	- After this further decreases in $P_\text{a}\ce{O2}$ results in a rapid decrease in $S_\text{a}\ce{O2}$ (~30%/min)
	- Factors affecting safe apnoea time include: 
		- Critical illness
		- Inadequate preoxygenation
		- Obesity
		- Pregnancy
		- Shunt physiology
		- Airway occlusion ([[#^fig-apnoea-airway-occlusion|Figure]])
			- During apnoea $\ce{O2}$ leaves the alveoli much faster than $\ce{CO2}$ enters them, so alveolar gas volume falls
			- With an open airway the deficit is replaced by bulk flow from the pharynx, so FRC is held (and refilled with $\ce{O2}$ if apnoeic oxygenation is used)
			- With an occluded airway nothing replaces it, so FRC falls below closing capacity → absorption atelectasis → shunt and a smaller $\ce{O2}$ store, hence faster desaturation
		- Increased oxygen consumption (e.g. high metabolic rate, fasciculations from suxamethonium)
		- Anaemia or dyshaemoglobinaemia
- Oxygenation can be further optimised by performing apnoeic oxygenation (e.g. 15 L/min via nasal prongs)

## Method
- Standard monitoring as that in [[Pre-Intubation]] and head up at ≥30° with auditory meatus above the jugular notch
- Ensure a patent airway
- Place standard nasal prongs at 15L/min prior to placement of pre-oxgenation if apnoeic oxygenation is desired
- Choose pre-oxgenation device based on $S_\text{p}\ce{O2}$:
	- If $S_\text{p}\ce{O2} > 95\%$ use:
		- Bag valve mask with PEEP valve and good seal at 15+ L/min or
		- Non-rebreather mask and a good seal at 15+ L/min
	- If $S_\text{p}\ce{O2}<95\%$ use:
		- Bag valve mask with PEEP valve and good seal at 15+ L/min or
- If adequate respiratory drive, preoxygenate by:
	- At least 3 minutes of tidal ventilations or
	- 8 breaths with full inspiration/expiration to achieve vital capacity in <60 seconds (requires patient co-operation)
- If inadequate respiratory drive, preoxygenate by:
	- Positive pressure ventilation (e.g. with bag-valve mask)
# Securing
- Securing ETT for [[Neuroprotection]]
	- Secure the ET tube using cloth ties positioned superiorly above the ears and around the occiput
		- This avoids possible impairment to cerebral venous outflow by jugular vein compression
		- This is a rapid way to secure the airway in difficult conditions where movement may be common
