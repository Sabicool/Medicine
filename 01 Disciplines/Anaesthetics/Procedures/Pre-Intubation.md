# Examination
- 2 points of IV access with consideration given to more access ± arterial line in high-risk/unwell patients
- See [[Airway Assessment]]
- Echocardiography (see [[Echocardiogram]]) to review for hypovolaemia, RV dysfunction, LV dysfunction, critical aortic stenosis and/or tamponade
- Gastric ultrasound (see: [Gastric UltraSound • Image Acquisition](https://www.gastricultrasound.org/en/acquisition/))
# Optimise Oxygenation
- Risk factors for hypoxaemia:
	- Obesity or pregnancy
	- [[Pneumothorax]]
- Interventions to consider:
	- BiPAP/CPAP for recruitment
	- Inhaled pulmonary vasodilator for patients with RV failure or refractory hypoxaemia
	- Pleural interventions e.g. thoracocentesis, chest drain
# Optimise Haemodynamics
- Risk factors for peri-intubation arrest or hypotension:
	- Pre-intubation MAP < 75 mmHg
	- Shock index > 0.8
	- Underlying cardiac disease (e.g. aortic stenosis, right ventricular dysfunction, tamponade)
	- BMI > 25, pregnancy or [[Abdominal Compartment Syndrome]]
- Interventions to consider:
	- Noradrenaline or adrenaline 
	- Target MAP > 80 mmHg prior to intubation
	- Arterial line insertion
	- Avoid excessive intrathoracic pressure post-intubation:
		- Avoid excessive bag-valve mask ventilation prior to intubation
		- Set the ventilator with low PEEP (5cm) initially, then gradually titrate up (see [[Mechanical Ventilation]])
	- Treat hypovolaemia if truly present
	- In severe [[Hypocalcaemia]] consider giving IV calcium after intubation
		- Avoid calcium boluses prior as it can sometimes cause vomiting
	- Stepwise sedation
	- Treat RV failure with inhaled pulmonary vasodilators
# Optimise pH
- Risk factors for pH deterioration
	- Severe metabolic acidosis (e.g. bicarbonate < 10 mmol/L)
	- Robust respiratory compensation with a low $P_\text{a}\ce{CO2}$
- Interventions to consider:
	- Consider delaying intubation to allow correction of the underlying process
	- IV bicarbonate (give >10 minutes before intubation to allow $\ce{CO2}$ to be ventilated off)
	- Intubate with a large bore ETT to minimise airway resistance
# Address Aspiration Risk
- Risk factors for aspiration
	- Active GI pathology (e.g. GI bleed, obstruction, ileus)
	- Gastroparesis (e.g. diabetic)
	- Recent meal
	- GLP-1 agonist use
	- Morbid obesity
	- Pregnancy
	- Hiatal hernia
- Interventions to consider:
	- Gastric ultrasound (see above)
	- More upright intubation positioning
	- ? Erythromycin 250mg IV
	- NG drainage
	- Ultra-rapid sequence oesophageal intubation
