- Volume of distribution is a parameter referring to the distribution of a medication and is calculated by the formula:
$$
\text{Volume of Distribution }(V_{D})=\frac{\text{Loading Dose}}{\text{Plasma Drug Concentration }(C)}
$$
- If a drug is tightly bound to plasma proteins (e.g. warfarin), it will appear as though the medication is dissolved in a smaller volume
- The components of volume of distribution are the plasma volume and the ratio of fraction of the drug unbound in plasma to the fraction of the durg unbound in tissue:
$$
V_{D}=\text{Plasma Volume }(V_{P})+\frac{\text{Fraction unbound in plasma }(f_{u})}{\text{Fraction unbound in tissue }(f_{u_{T}})}\times \text{Tissue Volume }(V_{T})
$$
# Measuring Volume of Distribution
- Serial measurements are taken after a loading dose and plotted against a logarithmic scale
	- The line of best fit is extrapolated to $t_{0}$ to find the plasma concentration at $t_{0}$ ($C_{0}$)
	- This is used to calculate the volume of distribution ($V_{D}=\frac{\text{Loading Dose}}{C_{0}}$)
# Clinical Significance
- The clinical significance of volume of distribution is two fold:
	1. An appropriate loading dose allows reaching the desired steady state concentration faster (i.e. $\text{Loading Dose}=V_{D}\times \text{Target Plasma Concentration}$)
	2. Determines pharmacodynamics
