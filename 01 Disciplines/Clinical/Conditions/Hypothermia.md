# Classification
- Stage 1: mild hypothermia (conscious, shivering, core temperature 32-35°C)
- Stage 2: moderate hypothermia (impaired consciousness without shivering, core temperature 28-32°C)
- Stage 3: severe hypothermia (unconscious, vital signs present, core temperature 24-28°C)
- Stage 4: cardiac arrest or low flow state (no or minimal vital signs, core temperature <24°C)
- Stage 5: death, due to irreversible hypothermia (core temperature < 13.7°C)

# Hypothermia Management

## Definitions & Classification (Swiss / HT Staging)

- **Hypothermia:** core temperature < 35 °C.
- HT I – Mild (35–32 °C): conscious, shivering.
- HT II – Moderate (32–28 °C): altered consciousness, may stop shivering, atrial arrhythmias.
- HT III – Severe (28–24 °C): unconscious, non-shivering, may be in cardiac arrest.
- HT IV – Profound (< 24 °C): cardiac arrest, absent vital signs.

Classical clinical–temperature correlations:
- 32 °C – decreased HR/BP/RR/conscious state; shivering stops; muscle rigidity; atrial arrhythmias<cite-badge data-idx="0"></cite-badge>
- 28 °C – ventricular arrhythmias (including VF), fixed dilated pupils<cite-badge data-idx="1"></cite-badge>
- 26 °C – comatose, absent reflexes, apnoea<cite-badge data-idx="2"></cite-badge>
- 20 °C – asystole<cite-badge data-idx="3"></cite-badge>

## Initial Assessment & General Principles

- Measure true core temperature: **low-reading oesophageal, rectal, or bladder** thermometer. Tympanic and axillary routes underestimate in hypothermia<cite-badge data-idx="4"></cite-badge>
- **Handle gently** – movement/jerky transfer may precipitate VF in severe hypothermia<cite-badge data-idx="5"></cite-badge>
- Pulse check up to **60 seconds**; may need ultrasound to confirm organised cardiac activity if slow pulse and cold skin<cite-badge data-idx="6"></cite-badge>
- Do not assume death from signs such as fixed dilated pupils, rigor mortis, or dependent lividity – these are unreliable in hypothermia<cite-badge data-idx="7"></cite-badge>
- Remove wet clothing, dry, wrap in warm dry coverings; place in warm environment<cite-badge data-idx="8"></cite-badge>
- ABC stabilisation: oxygen (warm humidified if possible), IV access, cardiac monitoring, IV access and warmed fluids<cite-badge data-idx="9"></cite-badge>
- Identify cause: environmental exposure vs secondary (sepsis, hypothyroidism, hypoglycaemia, drugs/toxins, Wernicke's, trauma).

## Management by Severity

### Mild (HT I, 35–32 °C) – Passive external rewarming
- Remove wet clothing, insulation, warm room, hot sweet drinks if fully conscious; encourage shivering and activity.
- Active external if cold environment / slow to warm.

### Moderate (HT II, 32–28 °C) – Active external rewarming
- Forced-air surface warming (e.g., **Bair Hugger**) is first-line and raises core temperature effectively<cite-badge data-idx="10"></cite-badge>
- Warmed IV fluids at **40–42 °C** (up to 1–2 L boluses as needed)<cite-badge data-idx="11"></cite-badge>
- Warm humidified oxygen
- Adjuncts: bladder, gastric or peritoneal lavage with warmed saline (evidence limited but reasonable)<cite-badge data-idx="12"></cite-badge>

### Severe / Profound (HT III–IV, < 28 °C) or failure of external rewarming – Active internal/core rewarming
- **ECMO (preferably V-A ECMO)** is first-line for hypothermic cardiac arrest or haemodynamically unstable severe hypothermia<cite-badge data-idx="13"></cite-badge>
- **Extracorporeal life support (ECLS)** is recommended first-line for hypothermic cardiac arrest<cite-badge data-idx="14"></cite-badge>
- Alternative/bridge options: continuous arteriovenous rewarming (CAVR), continuous venovenous rewarming, intravascular temperature management catheters, CRRT, warmed haemodialysis, thoracic lavage (open or closed with chest tubes), mediastinal irrigation after thoracotomy<cite-badge data-idx="15"></cite-badge>
- Signs of **failure of external rewarming** prompting escalation: core temp not rising or falling, rising lactate, decreasing GCS, hypotension, ventricular dysrhythmias<cite-badge data-idx="16"></cite-badge>

## Hypothermic Cardiac Arrest – Specific ACLS

- **Resuscitate** – do not stop based on "signs of death" alone. Aggressive treatment until core temp ≥ 32 °C is reasonable unless clearly non-survivable (e.g., frozen chest, K⁺ > 10 mmol/L)<cite-badge data-idx="17"></cite-badge>
- CPR: standard compression depth/rate. Consider extended/low-flow CPR if transportation to ECLS centre required.
- **Defibrillation:**
  - Give up to **3 shocks** initially.
  - If VF persists after 3 shocks and core temp **< 30 °C, defer further shocks** until core temp > 30 °C<cite-badge data-idx="18"></cite-badge>
  - All shocks at **maximum power**<cite-badge data-idx="19"></cite-badge>
- **Drugs:**
  - **Withhold adrenaline and amiodarone if core temp < 30 °C**<cite-badge data-idx="20"></cite-badge>
  - If adrenaline used while 28–30 °C: **double the interval** (every 6–10 min instead of 3–5 min)<cite-badge data-idx="21"></cite-badge>
  - Resume standard drug dosing once normothermia (≥ 35 °C) achieved<cite-badge data-idx="22"></cite-badge>
- **Active core rewarming is the primary therapeutic modality** in hypothermic cardiac arrest<cite-badge data-idx="23"></cite-badge>
- Targeted rewarming on ECMO: rate up to **5 °C/h** to core temp ~30 °C or ROSC; venous–arterial gradient < 4 °C to limit haemolysis and neurologic injury<cite-badge data-idx="24"></cite-badge>
- **Serum potassium** helps predict futility – **K⁺ > 10 mmol/L** associated with very poor outcome; consider ceasing resuscitation<cite-badge data-idx="25"></cite-badge>
- **Avalanche-specific:** burial time, asphyxia vs hypothermia, serum K⁺ (≤8 mmol/L with burial ≤60 min and open airway → CPR), air pocket, core temp at extrication – use ICAR MedCom checklist to guide continuation/cessation<cite-badge data-idx="26"></cite-badge>

## Post-resuscitation / Ongoing Care

- Continue active rewarming to **normothermia (~37 °C)** for patients with spontaneous circulation and 33–36 °C at presentation<cite-badge data-idx="27"></cite-badge>
- After ROSC in hypothermic CA: target temperature management per local protocol<cite-badge data-idx="28"></cite-badge>
- Monitor for complications: rhabdomyolysis, pancreatitis, DIC, ARDS, electrolyte shifts (K⁺, Mg²⁺, phosphate), sepsis, frostbite/cold injury.
- Investigate for underlying cause if not clearly environmental (TFTs, lactate, cultures, glucose, VBG, CXR, CT head if trauma).

## Summary Table

| Temperature | Stage | Rewarming strategy | Key actions |
|---|---|---|---|
| 35–32 °C | Mild (HT I) | Passive external | Warm room, insulation, oral calories, encourage shivering |
| 32–28 °C | Moderate (HT II) | Active external | Bair Hugger, warmed IV fluids 40–42 °C, warm humidified O₂ |
| 28–24 °C | Severe (HT III) | Active core (± ECMO) | Minimally invasive ± escalate; ECMO if haemodynamically unstable |
| < 24 °C | Profound (HT IV) / cardiac arrest | ECMO (V-A) | Full CPR, ≤3 shocks then defer if T < 30 °C; withhold adrenaline/amiodarone if T < 30 °C; K⁺ > 10 mmol/L suggests futility |

⚠ Check potassium before cessation of resuscitation – K⁺ > 10 mmol/L is a strong marker of futility in hypothermic cardiac arrest.
⚠ Avoid rough movement in severe hypothermia – mechanical stimulation of a cold myocardium can precipitate VF.
⚠ Confirm temperature with a low-reading oesophageal, rectal or bladder probe; tympanic/axillary readings are unreliable.
