# Amiodarone
- Contraindicated in second‑ or third-degree heart block (without pacemaker), symptomatic bradycardia (without pacemaker) or sick sinus syndrome (without pacemaker).
- Check that QT interval is <500 milliseconds before use as amiodarone may increase risk of arrhythmia by prolonging the QT interval. Avoid use if risk factors for [prolonged QT interval](https://amhonline.amh.net.au.acs.hcn.com.au/chapters/cardiovascular-drugs/drugs-arrhythmias/arrhythmias#arrhythmias-0201) cannot be corrected

# Digoxin
## Loading in Renal Impairment
- Loading dose $=V_{D}\times$ target concentration, so clearance doesn't affect it (see [[Volume of Distribution#Clinical Significance]])
	- But $V_{D}$ falls in renal impairment, as uraemia reduces tissue binding (from about 7 L/kg to about 4–5 L/kg), so the load still needs reducing
- Jusko–Koup estimate, using lean body weight (digoxin doesn't distribute into fat):
$$
V_{D}\,(\text{L})\approx3.8\times\text{weight (kg)}+3.1\times\text{CrCl (mL/min)}
$$
- e.g. 70 kg lean, IV, target 0.8 µg/L (≈1 nmol/L):

| CrCl (mL/min) | $V_{D}$ | Loading dose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | ≈580 L | ≈460 mcg |
| 30 | ≈360 L | ≈290 mcg |
| Anuric | ≈270 L | ≈215 mcg |

- Rule of thumb: reduce the load by about 30–50% in severe CKD or ESRD
	- A higher target (e.g. 1.2 µg/L for rate control) scales the dose up proportionally
	- For an oral load, divide by bioavailability (about 0.7 for tablets)
## In Practice
- Give the load in divided doses, e.g. half, then a quarter at 6 h and a quarter at 12 h if needed
	- Before each step, reassess HR, ECG, K⁺ and a level (≥6 h after the dose)
	- In renal impairment it's often reasonable to give a single 250 mcg IV dose and reassess, rather than committing to a full load
- Reduce the maintenance dose much more than the load: clearance is about 70% renal, and $t_{1/2}$ goes from about 36 h to about 3.5–5 days in anuria
	- Without a load, steady state takes about 1 week with normal renal function and 2–3 weeks in ESRD, which is why a cautious load is still worth giving and why an overshoot lasts a long time
- Toxicity lasts longer in renal failure
	- Not meaningfully removed by dialysis because of its large $V_{D}$
	- After digoxin Fab, the Fab–digoxin complex is cleared slowly and free digoxin can rebound days later, so monitor for longer
- **Amiodarone** raises digoxin levels by about 70–100%, so halve the digoxin dose. This adds to the renal adjustment, and many ICU patients have both
