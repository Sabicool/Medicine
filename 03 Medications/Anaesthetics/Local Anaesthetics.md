# Agents
- Amides that block voltage-gated sodium channels from inside the cell
- **Lignocaine:** intermediate-acting; skin infiltration and local procedures
- **Levobupivacaine:** long-acting; nerve blocks and regional anaesthesia. The S-enantiomer of bupivacaine, so less cardiotoxic
- Work poorly in infected tissue as the acidosis leaves more drug ionised, which can't cross the membrane

> [!card]- Why do local anaesthetics work poorly in infected tissue?
> The acidic tissue leaves more drug ionised, and only the unionised form crosses the membrane to reach the sodium channel.

> [!cloze]
> Levobupivacaine is the ==S-enantiomer== of bupivacaine, so it's less cardiotoxic.

# Maximum Doses

| Drug        | Plain    | With adrenaline |
| ----------- | -------- | --------------- |
| Lignocaine  | 3 mg/kg  | 7 mg/kg         |
| Bupivacaine | 2 mg/kg  | 2 mg/kg         |
| Ropivacaine | 3 mg/kg  | 3 mg/kg         |
| Prilocaine  | 6 mg/kg  | 8 mg/kg         |

- Dose on lean body weight, since toxicity comes from the peak plasma level
- Doses of different local anaesthetics add up
- Use less in the elderly and in hepatic or cardiac impairment
- Strength in mg/mL is the % × 10 (e.g. 1% = 10 mg/mL)

# Toxicity
- Initial IV bolus of 1.5 mL/kg of 20% lipid emulsion over 1 minute followed by an infusion at 15 mL/kg/h
	- Give up to three bolus doses (two more) of lipid (1.5 mL/kg) at 5 minute intervals if:
		- Remains haemodynamically unstable
		- Adequate circulation deteriorates
	- Continue the infusion until the patient is stable, double the rate to 30 mL/kg/h at any time after 5 minutes if:
		- Haemodnyamically unstable
		- Adequate circulation deteriorates
	- Do not exceed a maximum dose of 12 mL/kg
