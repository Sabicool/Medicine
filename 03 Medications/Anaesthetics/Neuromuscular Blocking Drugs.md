# Non-Depolarising
- Competitive antagonists at nicotinic receptors at the neuromuscular junction, binding the α subunits
- Aminosteroids: rocuronium, vecuronium
- Benzylisoquinoliniums: atracurium, cisatracurium, mivacurium
	- Atracurium and cisatracurium undergo Hofmann elimination, so offset doesn't depend on renal or hepatic function
	- Mivacurium is broken down by plasma cholinesterase
- Doses: [gap]
- Reversal: neostigmine with glycopyrrolate (see [[Acetylcholinesterase Inhibitor]]), or sugammadex, which encapsulates rocuronium and vecuronium

> [!card]- How do non-depolarising muscle relaxants work?
> Competitive antagonism of ACh at the nicotinic receptor of the neuromuscular junction, binding the α subunits.

> [!cloze]
> Atracurium and cisatracurium undergo ==Hofmann elimination==, so they're the relaxants of choice in renal and hepatic failure.

> [!cloze]
> Sugammadex reverses ==rocuronium and vecuronium== by encapsulating them.

# Suxamethonium
- Depolarising, very rapid onset
- Chaotic depolarisation causes fasciculations (used as the intubation endpoint), then flaccid paralysis
- Short-acting as plasma cholinesterase breaks it down, so prolonged in cholinesterase deficiency
- Raises K⁺, which is dangerous in burns, denervation and prolonged immobility
- Malignant hyperthermia trigger
- Bradycardia, especially with a repeat dose

> [!cloze]
> Suxamethonium causes ==fasciculations== before flaccid paralysis, which is used as the endpoint for intubation.

> [!card]- When is suxamethonium dangerous?
> - Hyperkalaemia risk: burns, denervation, prolonged immobility
> - Malignant hyperthermia susceptibility
> - Plasma cholinesterase deficiency (prolonged paralysis)
