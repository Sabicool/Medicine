- Examples: Neostigmine and Pyridostigmine
- Neostigmine increases ACh at the neuromuscular junction, which outcompetes a non-depolarising blocker, so can be used for reversal of neuromuscular blockade (sugammadex is used for rocuronium or vecuronium)
	- Give simultaneously with anticholinergic agent, such as atropine or glycopyrronium, to prevent muscarinic effects (especially bradycardia)
	- Glycopyrrolate is usual as its onset matches neostigmine's, see [[Anticholinergics]]
- Used in myasthenia gravis as well

> [!card]- Why is glycopyrrolate given with neostigmine?
> Neostigmine raises ACh at muscarinic as well as nicotinic receptors, causing bradycardia. Glycopyrrolate blocks that, and its onset matches neostigmine's.
