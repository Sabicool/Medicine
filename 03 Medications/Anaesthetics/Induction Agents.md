# Propofol
- GABA$_A$ positive modulator, given as a lipid emulsion
- Dose: 1.5–2.5 mg/kg IV
- ↓ BP, ↓ stroke volume and ↓ cardiac output (~25%) with vasodilation
- Respiratory depression, bronchodilation
- Antiemetic
- Lipid emulsion counts towards calories in ICU (~1.1 kcal/mL)
- Propofol infusion syndrome with high-dose, prolonged infusions: metabolic acidosis, rhabdomyolysis, hyperkalaemia, arrhythmias and AKI

> [!cloze]
> Propofol drops cardiac output by about ==25%== through vasodilation and reduced stroke volume.

> [!cloze]
> Unlike most anaesthetic drugs, propofol is ==antiemetic==.

> [!card]- What are the features of propofol infusion syndrome?
> Metabolic acidosis, rhabdomyolysis, hyperkalaemia, arrhythmias and AKI, with high-dose prolonged infusions.

# Thiopentone
- Barbiturate (GABA$_A$)
- Dose: 3–7 mg/kg IV, lasting 5–10 min
	- Offset is from redistribution out of the brain into muscle and fat rather than metabolism, so repeated doses or an infusion accumulate
- ↓ BP, ↓ SVR, ↓ cardiac output with a reflex ↑ HR
- Used for status epilepticus and raised ICP (burst suppression)

> [!card]- Why does a single dose of thiopentone wear off in 5–10 minutes?
> Redistribution from the brain to muscle and fat, not metabolism. Hence it accumulates with repeated doses or an infusion.

# Ketamine
- Phencyclidine derivative, NMDA antagonist, causing dissociative anaesthesia
- Releases noradrenaline and adrenaline, so ↑ HR, ↑ cardiac output, ↑ BP
	- Underneath that it's a direct myocardial depressant, which shows when catecholamines are depleted, so BP can still fall in shocked patients. In the RSI trial, cardiovascular collapse was more common with ketamine than etomidate (see [[Rapid Sequence Induction]])
- Respiratory rate maintained or ↑, bronchodilation, so useful in asthma, trauma and hypotension with the caveat above
- Emergence phenomena (vivid dreams, agitation)

> [!cloze]
> Ketamine ==raises== HR, BP and cardiac output through catecholamine release, and causes bronchodilation.

> [!card]- Why can ketamine still drop BP in a shocked patient?
> Its rise in BP is indirect, from catecholamine release. With depleted catecholamines its direct myocardial depression is unmasked.

## Doses
- Induction: 1–2 mg/kg IV
- Analgesia: 0.2–0.5 mg/kg IV or 5–10 mg IV boluses titrated to effect
- Procedural sedation: 0.5–1 mg/kg IV
	- Consider adding ondansetron for dissociative procedural sedation in whom emesis could be harmful (e.g. spinal precautions, traumatic brain injury, ocular injury, penetrating abdominal injury)
- Delayed sequence induction: 1 mg/kg IV or 1–3 mg/kg IM

> [!NOTE] Dose reduce with comorbidities
> Dose reduce for:
> - Patients with shock or significant haemodynamic compromise
> - Hepatic impairment
> - Frailty or advanced age

# Midazolam
- Benzodiazepine, GABA$_A$ positive modulator
- Dose: 0.02–0.1 mg/kg IV
- Sedation, anxiolysis and anterograde amnesia
- Reversed by flumazenil
- Accumulates with prolonged infusions and in renal failure (active glucuronide metabolite)

> [!card]- Why does a midazolam infusion wear off slowly in renal failure?
> Its active glucuronide metabolite is renally cleared, so it accumulates.
